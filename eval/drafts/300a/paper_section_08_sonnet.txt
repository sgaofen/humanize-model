**Results**

Tensile properties of Ti-6Al-4V specimens fabricated via laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) were evaluated across five laser power settings (150 W, 175 W, 200 W, 225 W, and 250 W) on an EOS M290 system, with scan speed (1200 mm/s), hatch spacing (0.10 mm), and layer thickness (30 μm) held constant. Ten dog-bone specimens (ASTM E8 subsize geometry) were printed per condition, stress-relieved at 650°C for 3 h under argon, and tested to failure on an Instron 5985 at a crosshead speed of 1 mm/min.

Ultimate tensile strength (UTS) rose from 1021 ± 18 MPa at 150 W to a peak of 1147 ± 12 MPa at 200 W, before declining to 1089 ± 24 MPa at 225 W and 1043 ± 31 MPa at 250 W (mean ± SD, n = 10). A one-way ANOVA confirmed a significant effect of laser power on UTS (F(4,45) = 26.7, p < 0.001); Tukey HSD post hoc comparisons showed the 200 W group differed significantly from all other groups (p < 0.01) except 225 W (p = 0.08). Yield strength followed the same trend, peaking at 1068 ± 15 MPa at 200 W versus 948 ± 21 MPa at 150 W. Elongation at break was highest at 200 W (11.4 ± 0.9%) and dropped sharply at 250 W (6.2 ± 1.3%), coinciding with increased porosity (0.8% vs. 2.9% by micro-CT, respectively) attributed to keyhole-mode instability at higher energy density.

Fractographic analysis (SEM, Zeiss Sigma 300) of 250 W specimens revealed spherical keyhole pores (30–80 μm diameter) concentrated near the fracture initiation site, consistent with the reduced ductility observed. In contrast, 150 W specimens exhibited lack-of-fusion defects with irregular morphology, correlating with lower UTS despite comparable overall porosity (1.1%). These results indicate 200 W as the optimal setting within the tested range for maximizing strength-ductility balance under the fixed scan parameters used in this study.
