# How I Saved $4,200 on Three Weeks in Portugal — Without Roughing It

When I booked my trip to Portugal this past January, the sticker price should have been around $6,800. Instead, I paid about $2,600 total. Here's exactly how it happened.

**The flights: $1,850 saved**

I'd been sitting on 68,000 Chase Sapphire Preferred points and a stash of United miles from work travel over the past two years. In mid-September, I saw a transfer bonus: Chase was offering 25% extra points when transferred to United. That turned my points into enough for a round-trip saver award from Newark to Lisbon. I paid $121 in taxes and fees for a ticket that was selling for $982 in February — my travel dates.

The key was timing. I flew out January 9th and returned January 30th. Post-holiday, mid-winter fares to Europe from the Northeast drop dramatically. The same itinerary in June was pricing at over $1,400.

**Accommodation: $1,600 saved**

I split my three weeks between Lisbon (8 nights), Porto (6 nights), and Lagos in the Algarve (7 nights). Instead of hotels, I booked apartments through Booking.com in January — peak off-season, when owners are hungry for any booking. My one-bedroom apartment in Lisbon's Alfama district was $54/night. A comparable June rate? Around $150.

In Lagos, I found a guesthouse with an ocean-view room for $47/night, breakfast included. I also stacked this with a credit card portal: booking through the Chase travel portal with my Sapphire card effectively earned me another 5% back in points.

**Everything else: $750+ saved**

- Museums: The Lisbon Card (€42 for 72 hours) covered the Jerónimos Monastery, Belém Tower, and public transit — about €60 in value.
- Off-season tours: A Douro Valley wine tour from Porto cost €55 instead of the €85 summer price, and there were only six of us instead of a full van.
- Food: January meant no reservations needed at Taberna da Rua das Flores in Lisbon — I walked in on a Tuesday. Same dinner, no tourist markup.

**The trade-offs (being honest)**

It rained four days in Porto. Some beach restaurants in Lagos were closed. Days were short — sunset around 5:30pm. But Portugal in January still hit 60°F in Lisbon most afternoons, and I had Sintra's Pena Palace nearly to myself on a Wednesday morning. In summer, that's a two-hour line.

**The math**

Flights: $1,850 saved. Lodging: $1,600. Tours and attractions: $750. Total: $4,200 — enough to fund my next trip, which I'm already plotting. Greece in November, anyone?
