I signed up for the six-week beginner wheel course at Clayworks on Fulton Street on a whim, two days after turning 38. My husband bought it as a joke birthday present. I did not think I would still be there fourteen months later.

The first bowl I ever centered took forty minutes and came out looking like a beret. My teacher, a soft-spoken guy named Marcus who'd been throwing for twenty years, told me centering is ninety percent of the fight and nobody gets it in week one. He was being kind. I didn't get it until week nine.

For the first four months, everything I made leaned. I have a mug from October that tilts so badly it looks like it's mid-pour even sitting still. I kept it on my desk as a reminder instead of throwing it out. My wife started calling my early pieces "the Leaning Tower of Ceramics."

The turn came in February, on a Tuesday night class, when I threw a set of four cereal bowls that actually matched — same height, same wall thickness, walls thin enough to see light through the greenware. Marcus held one up and said, "Now you're not fighting the clay anymore." I went home and cried a little, which felt absurd for a bowl.

By June I had a glaze I liked — a break-y iron-speckled white called Shino that does something different every firing depending on where it sits in the kiln — and forty pieces sitting on a shelf with nowhere to go. My daughter, who's ten, suggested the farmers market on Saturdays at Wallingford, the one by our house. I paid $35 for a half-table for one morning in August.

I sold eleven pieces. Three mugs, four small bowls, a butter dish, and three of the speckled cereal bowls I was proudest of, all to a woman who said she was replacing her entire set. I made $187 and spent about four times that on class fees this year, so it is not a business. But I drove home with an empty box in the trunk instead of a full one, and that felt like something.

I'm signed up for an intermediate class starting in October. Marcus says it's time I learned to trim properly. I still have the beret bowl. It's holding my keys now.
