At the Sunrise Diner on Route 9 in Millbrook, we've run the numbers, and $15 an hour would close us within two years.

I've waited tables at the Sunrise since I was sixteen, and my mother, Linda, has owned it since 1994. We employ eleven people: six servers, three cooks, a dishwasher, and a part-time bookkeeper. Right now our cooks make $13.50 an hour and our servers make $8 plus tips, which usually brings them to $16-19 an hour on a Saturday and closer to $11 on a slow Tuesday in February. Raising the base wage to $15 flat sounds fair on paper, but the diner clears about $40,000 in profit a year on $680,000 in revenue — a margin under 6%, which is normal for a place like ours. A $15 minimum, once you add the payroll taxes and workers' comp that scale with wages, would cost us roughly $58,000 more a year. That's not profit we're sitting on; that's the whole cushion, gone, plus more.

The counterargument matters too, and I don't dismiss it. Chris, our line cook, supports a two-year-old on $13.50 an hour, and no one can call that a living wage in 2026, even in a town where a two-bedroom rents for $950. If Chris worked at the Dollar General two miles down the road, Ohio's state minimum would already be pushing past $10.70, and a federal $15 floor would give him real, immediate relief without him needing to organize or wait on us to volunteer it. Proponents like the Economic Policy Institute have argued for over a decade that $15 restores wages to their 1968 purchasing power. That's not a fantasy number; it's a real benchmark.

What we need isn't a blanket national wage but a regional one, or a slower phase-in — something like California's stepped approach from 2017 to 2022, which gave small employers time to raise menu prices gradually instead of all at once. A four-dollar jump in one year forces us to either cut Chris's hours, raise a cup of coffee to $4, or close. None of those help him either. A wage tied to local cost of living, phased in over four or five years, would let Millbrook catch up without every diner on Route 9 going dark first.
