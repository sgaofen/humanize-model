On January 8, 2025, at age 51, I laced up my running shoes with one goal: to finish my first half marathon after a knee replacement.

The idea would have sounded ridiculous a year earlier. In March 2024, I had my right knee replaced after years of arthritis made stairs, long walks, and even getting out of the car miserable. Recovery was slow and humbling. My physical therapist, Elena Morales, started me with five-minute walks around my neighborhood in Sacramento, California. By June, I could walk two miles without stopping. By September, I was riding a stationary bike and doing strength exercises three times a week.

Running still felt off-limits. Then my friend Marcus mentioned the Davis Turkey Trot Half Marathon, scheduled for November 27, 2025. “You don’t have to run every step,” he said. “You just have to train consistently.”

So I began with a walk-run plan: one minute of jogging followed by two minutes of walking, repeated for 20 minutes. My first workout covered just 1.4 miles, and my knee was swollen afterward. I iced it, called Elena, and learned an important lesson: progress is not the same as pushing through pain.

Now I train four days a week. Tuesdays are easy walk-runs, Thursdays are strength sessions, Saturdays are longer, slower outings, and Sundays are for stretching and recovery. I increase my long distance by no more than half a mile each week. In February, I completed three miles. By April, I reached six. Last Saturday, I finished nine miles along the American River Parkway in two hours and eleven minutes.

I am not chasing a personal record. My goal is to cross the finish line in Davis before the course closes, preferably smiling and still able to walk afterward. I know my pace will be slower than it was when I was 30—mostly because I never ran when I was 30. But every mile feels like proof that my body is still capable of surprising me.

At 51, with a titanium knee and a very patient training plan, I’m learning that starting over can be its own kind of victory.
