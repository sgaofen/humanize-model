Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has undergone a rapid transformation since 10x Genomics launched the Chromium Single Cell Controller in Pleasanton, California, on October 18, 2016. By combining microfluidic droplets, barcoded gel beads, and unique molecular identifiers, Chromium made it practical to profile thousands of individual cells in parallel, substantially increasing throughput while reducing the cost and labor associated with earlier plate-based methods such as Smart-seq2. This technological shift coincided with the launch of the Human Cell Atlas initiative at the Wellcome Trust in London on October 13–14, 2016, which established a large-scale framework for defining human cell types and states across tissues. [10x Genomics](https://s28.q4cdn.com/592666581/files/doc_news/2016/10/18/10x-genomics-announces-new-low-cost-single-cell-instrument.pdf), [Human Cell Atlas](https://www.humancellatlas.org/event/human-cell-atlas-launch-meeting/)

Since then, scRNA-seq has expanded in both scale and biological scope. Improved droplet chemistry, sequencing protocols, and computational pipelines have enabled studies of hundreds of thousands to millions of cells, while single-nucleus RNA sequencing has extended analysis to frozen, archived, and structurally complex tissues that are difficult to dissociate intact. Parallel developments have linked transcriptomes with other molecular layers, including chromatin accessibility, DNA methylation, protein abundance, and immune-receptor sequences. These multimodal approaches have helped resolve cellular states, developmental trajectories, tumor heterogeneity, and tissue-specific regulatory programs more precisely than bulk RNA sequencing. [Nature Reviews Genetics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00580-2)

More recently, spatial transcriptomics has addressed a central limitation of dissociative scRNA-seq: the loss of anatomical context. By retaining information about where transcripts are expressed, spatial methods connect molecular cell identities to tissue architecture and cell–cell interactions. Together, high-throughput sequencing, multimodal profiling, spatial measurement, and increasingly sophisticated statistical and machine-learning methods have shifted scRNA-seq from a specialized measurement technology into a foundational platform for constructing cellular atlases and investigating disease mechanisms.
