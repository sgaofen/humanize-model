When we began homeschooling our son, Eli, in September 2022, he was eight years old and reading at roughly a first-grade level. Dyslexia made every worksheet feel like a test he had already failed. He could explain the water cycle with a detailed drawing, but reading a short paragraph about it left him exhausted and embarrassed.

We tried three popular language arts programs during that first year. One relied heavily on independent reading, and another moved through spelling lists too quickly. Neither lasted more than six weeks. The curriculum that finally worked for us was All About Reading Level 2, paired with All About Spelling. The lessons were short—about 20 minutes each—and completely explicit about phonograms, syllables, and blending. Eli used letter tiles, tapped out sounds on the table, and read decodable books instead of guessing from pictures. By February 2023, he was reading simple chapter books aloud without shutting down.

For writing, we used Institute for Excellence in Writing’s Primary Arts of Language. I appreciated that it separated the physical act of writing from the work of generating ideas. At first, Eli dictated his answers to me while I typed them. Gradually, he copied one sentence, then two, and eventually wrote short paragraphs. His favorite assignment was retelling “The Three Little Pigs” from the wolf’s point of view.

Math was less of a struggle, but word problems were difficult because reading interfered with showing what he knew. Math-U-See worked well because it was hands-on and predictable. We spent nearly two months on multiplication with colorful blocks before moving to written equations. I read every word problem aloud, and he was allowed to draw the problem or explain his reasoning verbally.

The biggest change was slowing down. We school for about three focused hours each morning, with movement breaks every 25 minutes. We also stopped comparing our progress with the local school calendar. In May 2024, Eli read an entire 120-page adventure book independently. It took him nearly three weeks, but he finished it—and immediately asked for the sequel.

Dyslexia has not disappeared, and homeschooling has not become effortless. But the right curricula gave Eli tools instead of pressure. More importantly, they helped him see himself as capable.
