Christopher Nolan’s *Oppenheimer* (2023) is an intense, ambitious film that feels especially powerful in IMAX. The huge format makes the Trinity test sequence genuinely overwhelming: the silence before the explosion, the sudden flash of light, and the delayed roar create a physical sense of dread rather than simple spectacle. Ludwig Göransson’s score and the pounding sound design add to the experience without making it feel like a conventional action movie.

Cillian Murphy gives a remarkable performance as J. Robert Oppenheimer, portraying him as brilliant, vain, anxious, and increasingly haunted by the consequences of his work. The supporting cast is exceptional too, particularly Robert Downey Jr. as Lewis Strauss, Emily Blunt as Kitty Oppenheimer, and Matt Damon as General Leslie Groves. The film’s shifting timelines and black-and-white sections initially feel complicated, but they gradually build a compelling picture of how scientific achievement became entangled with politics, ego, and fear.

At three hours, *Oppenheimer* is undeniably long. Some courtroom and security-hearing scenes move slowly, and the dense dialogue demands concentration. Still, the runtime feels mostly justified because the film is covering the Manhattan Project, Oppenheimer’s personal life, and the political fallout over several decades. In IMAX, the scale and detail make the length easier to accept, though it is worth taking a break beforehand. This is not a relaxed historical drama; it is an absorbing, exhausting experience that lingers long after the credits.
