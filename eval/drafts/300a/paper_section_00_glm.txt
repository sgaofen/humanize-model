**Abstract**

The hepatic uptake transporter OATP1B1, encoded by *SLCO1B1*, mediates the uptake of numerous endogenous compounds and clinically important drugs, including statins, into hepatocytes. Common loss-of-function variants in *SLCO1B1*, such as the rs4149056 T&gt;C polymorphism (c.521T&gt;C, Val174Ala), are associated with increased systemic statin exposure and elevated risk of myopathy, yet the quantitative contribution of OATP1B1 to total hepatocellular uptake of individual substrates remains incompletely characterized. Here, we generated a CRISPR-Cas9-mediated knockout of *SLCO1B1* in HepG2 cells to directly quantify the transporter's contribution to hepatic uptake. Using two single-guide RNAs targeting exons 2 and 6 of *SLCO1B1*, we established clonal knockout lines confirmed by Sanger sequencing, RT-qPCR demonstrating &gt;95% reduction in *SLCO1B1* transcript abundance, and absence of OATP1B1 protein by Western blot. Whole-genome-off-target assessment via targeted amplicon sequencing of the top ten predicted off-target loci revealed no indels exceeding 1.2% allele frequency. Uptake assays performed at 37°C over 5 minutes using fluorescently labeled pravastatin (10 µM) showed a 43.0% ± 3.8% reduction (mean ± SEM, *n* = 4 independent experiments, *p* &lt; 0.001, two-tailed unpaired *t*-test) in uptake in knockout clones relative to wild-type HepG2 cells and non-targeting guide RNA controls. The residual uptake was insensitive to the OATP inhibitor rifampicin (100 µM) but partially inhibited by probenecid, suggesting mediation by additional organic anion transport systems, likely including OATP1B3 and Na⁺-taurocholate cotransporting polypeptide, both of which remained expressed in the knockout clones. Kinetic analysis indicated that the reduction in uptake reflected a 46% decrease in maximal transport capacity (Vmax: 285 ± 31 vs. 528 ± 44 pmol/mg protein/5 min) without a significant change in apparent Km. These findings provide a direct quantitative estimate of OATP1B1's contribution to statin uptake in a human hepatocyte model and establish a validated isogenic knockout platform suitable for screening substrate- and genotype-dependent drug disposition, with implications for precision dosing of OATP1B1 substrates in carriers of loss-of-function alleles.
