I still remember the exact price of the first album I bought with my own money: $14.99 at the Tower Records on Sunset Boulevard, April 2003. It was Radiohead's *Hail to the Thief*, and I played it so many times that summer that I can still hear the seam between "2 + 2 = 5" and "Sit Down. Stand Up." — the specific way one track bled into the next.

That's the thing we lost. Not the money, though $14.99 was real money for a fourteen-year-old. We lost the *seam*. The transition. The moment where a band decided what came after what.

Streaming gave us everything — 100 million-plus tracks on Spotify as of 2024 — and in doing so it made sequence irrelevant. When music became infinite, the playlist became an algorithmic slot machine. Spotify's Discover Weekly drops thirty songs into your ears every Monday, and studies suggest most listeners skip a track within the first five to thirty seconds. We don't listen to songs anymore. We audition them. Constantly. Forever.

An album used to be an argument. *Abbey Road* builds to "The End." *OK Computer* places "Fitter Happier" exactly where it needs to be, a dead-eyed interlude between "Karma Police" and "Electioneering." Even a mixtape — and I made dozens in high school, laboriously, on a Sony dual-deck — was a document of care. You thought about what followed what. The order was the message. Someone could read your soul in the distance between a Radiohead track and the Björk song you put after it.

Now the queue is shuffled by default. "Enhance" adds songs the machine thinks fit. Skip, skip, skip. The average Spotify listener circles the same forty-some tracks week after week despite the infinite library — infinite choice produced narrower taste, not wider.

I don't want to romanticize the past. Streaming is a miracle in many ways: a kid in Fresno can hear Fela Kuti and Fugazi the same afternoon, free. But abundance came with a hidden cost. Music stopped being something you committed to and became something that flows past you, like weather. Songs used to be events. Now they're ambient.

Here's my small rebellion: every Friday night I put on one full album, front to back, no phone, no skipping. Last week it was *Blue Rev* by Alvvays. That fifty-one minutes felt more like listening than the previous six months combined.

The seams matter. We just forgot to notice when they disappeared.
