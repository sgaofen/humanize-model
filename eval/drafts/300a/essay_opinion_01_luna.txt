Times Square should be car-free before 2030—not as a stunt, but as a sensible next step in New York’s long experiment with reclaiming public space.

The transformation has already begun. In 2009, under Mayor Michael Bloomberg, Broadway was closed to traffic through Times Square and temporary pedestrian plazas appeared between 42nd and 47th Streets. What looked radical at the time quickly became ordinary. The plazas reduced vehicle conflicts, gave pedestrians more room, and created space for tables, performances, and the simple act of standing still in one of the world’s busiest neighborhoods.

Yet Times Square remains a contradiction: a place designed to overwhelm the senses, where millions of people gather each year, but where cars and trucks still cut through the crowds. More than 300,000 pedestrians pass through the area daily. They navigate between barricades, delivery vehicles, taxis, buses, and turning traffic while looking up at advertisements that sell New York as a city of possibility.

A permanent car-free Times Square would not mean banning every vehicle. Emergency responders, maintenance crews, accessibility services, and carefully scheduled deliveries could retain access. But private cars and routine through-traffic should go. The surrounding avenues—Seventh Avenue, Sixth Avenue, and Eighth Avenue—can carry necessary movement, while the central plaza becomes a genuinely civic space rather than a pedestrian island surrounded by machines.

The argument is not merely aesthetic. Cleaner air, fewer crashes, less noise, and safer crossings matter in a city where children, older adults, workers, and visitors all share limited space. Times Square is also one of the few places on earth with enough symbolic power to make a public statement: New York values people more than the convenience of driving across Midtown.

By 2030, the city should finish what it started in 2009. Times Square should belong first to the people who walk through it, work in it, perform in it, and make it feel alive. Cars have plenty of roads. One square should be allowed to breathe.
