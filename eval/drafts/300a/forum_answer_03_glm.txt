Short answer: yes, soreness at 6 weeks post-ACL reconstruction is completely normal — but the *type* of soreness matters.

I'm 4 months out from my hamstring graft ACL reconstruction (surgery done at Johns Hopkins on Feb 14), and at the 6-week mark I still had daily aching, especially around the incision sites and along the inside of my knee where they harvested the hamstring tendon. My physical therapist said that's expected because at 6 weeks the graft is in its weakest phase of healing (the "ligamentization" period), and you're likely ramping up activity — more walking, stationary bike, maybe starting leg presses — so the tissue is getting stressed in new ways.

What's normal at 6 weeks:

- Dull aching after PT sessions or long days on your feet
- Soreness around the portal incisions and donor site
- Stiffness after sitting in one position for a while
- Mild swelling that goes down overnight

What's NOT normal and warrants a call to your surgeon:

- Sharp, sudden pain, especially with a pivoting motion
- Significant new swelling that doesn't improve with ice and elevation
- Warmth, redness, or fever (possible infection — don't wait on this)
- Knee instability or a feeling of the knee "giving way"
- Calf pain, which could indicate a blood clot

My surgeon's rule of thumb: pain should be gradually trending better week over week, even if it fluctuates day to day. If your soreness is staying flat or getting worse over a 2-week stretch, get it checked.

A few things that helped me: icing 15–20 minutes after every PT session, keeping up with quad sets and heel slides even on sore days (motion helps), and sleeping with a pillow under the knee. Also make sure you're not limping — compensating with your hip causes its own soreness that gets confused with knee pain.

That said, I'm a random person on the internet, not your surgeon. If anything feels off, call their office — most have a nurse line and would much rather answer a "silly" question than miss a complication. Six weeks is still early; most people don't feel truly "good" until 3–4 months.
