1/ In June 2024, I walked away from a $180K software engineering job at a FAANG company in San Francisco.

On paper, it looked like the dream: great salary, smart colleagues, free meals, and a polished office overlooking the Bay. But most days, I couldn’t explain why my work mattered.

2/ That September, I started teaching computer science at Roosevelt High School in Sacramento.

My classroom has 32 students, six working laptops, and a budget that makes “cloud computing” sound like science fiction. But when Maya’s Python program finally ran after three days of debugging, she jumped out of her chair and yelled, “I made that!”

I felt more useful in that moment than I had in years.

3/ I didn’t leave because tech was bad. I left because I wanted my work to reach people, not just dashboards.

Teaching is harder than engineering in ways I never expected: the hours are longer, the resources are tighter, and the wins are often invisible.

But every day, I get to help young people see themselves as builders—not just consumers of technology.

That feels worth more than $180K.
