Last June, my mom turned 65 and instead of a party, she asked for something better: eleven days walking the Tour du Mont Blanc together. Not the whole 170 km—she was clear about that from the start. We'd do the classic French and Italian sections, skip a few stages with bus transfers, and average about 12 km a day instead of the usual 20-plus. It was the smartest decision we made.

The TMB forums are full of ultralight evangelists and 5 a.m. starters. Ignore them, mostly. What actually worked for us:

**Packing.** Mom carried 7 kg, I carried 10 (including the shared first aid kit and the wine we kept buying in Courmayeur). Her setup: one 40-liter pack, two merino hiking shirts, one pair of convertible pants, rain shell, down jacket, and—non-negotiable—her own trekking poles. She'd had a knee replacement in 2021 and swore the poles added five years back to her legs. I brought way too many shirts and sent half home from Chamonix.

**Pacing.** Our rule was one big climb per day, then stop. The climb out of Les Contamines to Col du Bonhomme (2,329 m) nearly broke us on day three, and we restructured everything after that. Start by 8:30, lunch at a refuge around noon—Order the tartiflette, always—and finish walking by 3:30 so Mom had a full hour to stretch and shower before aperitif. Refuges like Refuge Nant Borrant and Rifugio Bonatti book out months ahead for July, so reserve by March.

**The honest bits.** She cried at the Fenêtre d'Arpette viewpoint, and honestly so did I. There were also two days she took the bus while I walked (Les Chapieux to Courmayeur, no regrets—she drank espresso in town while I slogged over the Col de la Seigne in rain). The lesson: pacing isn't defeat. It's what made the trip possible at all.

If you're considering this with an older parent, book the flexible version of everything. And bring a deck of cards—refuge evenings are long, and Mom annihilated me at gin rummy every single night.

Would I do it again? We've already penciled in the Walker's Haute Route for her 70th. She says she'll train this time. She won't. It'll be fine.
