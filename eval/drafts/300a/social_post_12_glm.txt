Last summer, I had the privilege of mentoring three first-generation college interns at Deloitte's Chicago office — and they taught me more than I taught them.

Maria, a junior at UIC studying accounting, arrived on day one unsure she belonged. By August, she was presenting a process improvement proposal to our entire advisory team. Jonathan, a DePaul statistics major, built an Excel automation tool that saved our client team an estimated 10 hours a week. And Priya, a Loyola sophomore, turned her fresh perspective into a client deliverable that earned a direct shout-out from the engagement partner.

Here's what people don't always understand about first-gen students: they bring a resourcefulness you can't teach. When you've navigated financial aid forms, family obligations, and campus life without a roadmap, corporate ambiguity doesn't scare you.

But they shouldn't have to do it alone. Over 12 weeks, we covered everything from executive presence to negotiating job offers — the "hidden curriculum" that so many of us learned through luck or connections.

All three received return offers. All three accepted.

To every leader wondering if mentorship is worth the time: I spent maybe 3 hours a week. The return on that investment? Immeasurable.

This summer, I'm mentoring four. Let's keep opening doors.
