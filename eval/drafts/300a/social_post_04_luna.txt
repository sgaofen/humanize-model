1/ I paid off $38,000 in student loans in 26 months. I started in January 2022, while living in Denver and earning $68,000 a year. My minimum payments were $412/month, but interest kept the balance moving slowly.

2/ First, I listed every expense for 30 days. I cut $280/month by canceling subscriptions, cooking at home, and moving from a $1,650 apartment to a $1,150 roommate situation in Capitol Hill.

3/ I kept $1,000 for emergencies, then sent every extra dollar to the highest-interest loan. My target was $1,500/month, so I picked up freelance copywriting nights and weekends, averaging another $700–$1,000 monthly.

4/ I used three rules: no new credit-card debt, 24-hour waits before nonessential purchases, and automatic payments the day after payday. Tax refunds, bonuses, and freelance checks went straight to the loans.

5/ The hardest month was October 2022, when my car needed $1,200 in repairs. I paused extra payments for one month, used savings, and restarted in November. Progress wasn’t perfectly linear, but the system kept me moving.

6/ On February 29, 2024, I made the final $1,847 payment. The best part wasn’t the screenshot showing a $0 balance—it was redirecting that $1,500/month toward investing, travel, and breathing room.
