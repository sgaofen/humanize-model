Turn off the hot and cold shutoff valves under the sink and open the faucet to relieve pressure. Plug the drain so you don’t lose the retaining clip or screws.

For a common Moen single-handle faucet using the 1225 cartridge, remove the handle first. Depending on the handle, this usually means loosening a small Phillips screw or a 3/32-inch Allen screw. Lift off the handle, then remove the decorative sleeve and the retaining clip with needle-nose pliers. Take a photo before removing the clip so you reinstall it in the same orientation.

Pulling the old cartridge is often the difficult part. Use a Moen cartridge puller if it is stuck; twisting the cartridge back and forth with pliers can damage the faucet body. Clean mineral deposits from the valve opening, then apply a thin coat of silicone plumber’s grease to the O-rings of the replacement 1225 cartridge. Do not use petroleum grease.

Insert the new cartridge with the hot/cold orientation matching the old one—on many Moen faucets, the stem’s notch faces upward. Reinstall the retaining clip completely; the faucet may leak or the cartridge may eject if the clip is not seated. Reassemble the handle, turn the shutoff valves on slowly, and check for leaks.

If it still drips, confirm that the cartridge is fully inserted and that the retaining clip is installed correctly. A damaged valve body, cracked cartridge, or debris lodged behind the cartridge may require additional repair. Genuine Moen 1225 cartridges are widely available at hardware stores, and Moen’s lifetime warranty may provide a free replacement if the faucet qualifies.
