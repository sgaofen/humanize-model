I tried three recipes from Sarah Owens's *Sourdough* (Roost Books, 2015) over a rainy weekend in late August, working off a starter I'd fed for exactly ten days beforehand.

The basic country loaf (p. 44) was the standout — 72% hydration, a 4-hour bulk ferment at 78°F, and it came out of my Dutch oven with real ear and open crumb on the first try. No fuss, no failure.

The whole wheat sandwich loaf (p. 88) was fine but dense; I suspect my starter wasn't vigorous enough yet, since the book doesn't flag how much that recipe depends on a starter that's been established for at least three weeks, not the ten days I gave mine.

The cinnamon-raisin swirl (p. 156) was a disaster — the dough tore every time I tried to shape it, and the instructions skip the lamination step entirely, just saying "roll and fill," which left me with raisins bursting through the crust in the oven.

Where the book earns its keep is the troubleshooting appendix (pp. 210–215), which correctly diagnosed my gummy interior as underbaking, not overproofing, and got my second country loaf even better than the first.

Verdict: buy it for the country loaf and the troubleshooting section alone — those two chapters are worth the $24.95 cover price. Skip the enriched doughs until you've got a starter with a few more weeks on it, and don't attempt the swirl loaf without outside help; I ended up watching a YouTube tutorial to fix my lamination technique before it worked.
