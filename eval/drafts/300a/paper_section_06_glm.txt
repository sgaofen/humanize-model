# Introduction

In September 2021, the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye (CBRT) initiated a series of unorthodox policy rate cuts that would come to define one of the most consequential monetary policy experiments of the modern era. Over roughly four months, the policy rate was reduced from 19% to 14%, despite annual consumer price inflation already running near 20%. This trajectory continued into 2022 and 2023, with the policy rate falling as low as 8.5% by February 2023, even as headline inflation accelerated past 80% in October 2022—the highest reading in Türkiye's republican history. The intellectual justification, articulated repeatedly by President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and embedded in the government's economic program announced in December 2021, rested on the conviction that high interest rates are themselves a cause of inflation, an inversion of conventional monetary theory that came to be known in Turkish public discourse as " faiz sebep, enflasyon sonuç" (interest is the cause, inflation is the result).

This episode offers a rare natural experiment in which a large, internationally integrated economy deliberately pursued a policy course diametrically opposed to the inflation-targeting consensus, at a moment when global conditions—the post-pandemic demand recovery and the commodity price shock following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in February 2022—were already pushing inflation upward worldwide. Disentangling the domestic policy contribution from these global forces constitutes the central empirical challenge of this paper.

We contribute to the literature in three ways. First, we construct a monthly dataset spanning January 2010 through December 2023, combining CBRT policy decisions, Turkish Statistical Institute (TÜİK) price indices, sovereign credit default swap spreads, and USD/TRY exchange rate movements, to document the timing and transmission of the unorthodox easing cycle. Second, we employ a structural vector autoregression framework, supplemented by a synthetic control comparison against a weighted basket of emerging-market peers (including Brazil, South Africa, and Poland), to estimate the counterfactual inflation path absent the rate cuts. Third, we examine the behavioral dimension: survey evidence from TÜİK's consumer expectation surveys and CBRT market participant polls reveals how inflation expectations unanchored progressively during this period, with 12-month-ahead expectations exceeding 60% by mid-2022.

Our findings speak to ongoing debates about the costs of abandoning institutional monetary frameworks, the exchange rate pass-through channel in highly dollarized economies, and the political economy of central bank independence. The Turkish case, culminating in the return to orthodox policy under Governor Hafize Gaye Erkan in June 2023—with rates raised from 8.5% to 40% within six months—provides a cautionary natural experiment whose lessons extend well beyond Türkiye's borders.
