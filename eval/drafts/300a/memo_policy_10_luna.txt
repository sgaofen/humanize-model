As of September 21, 2026, 62% of our rare-earth components are sourced from Shenzhen-based vendor Shenzhen Huanyu Materials Co. This concentration creates a material supply-chain risk for products assembled at our San Jose, California, facility, particularly components containing neodymium, dysprosium, and terbium used in motors and high-efficiency magnets.

The primary exposure is disruption at a single supplier, whether caused by export controls, port congestion, power interruptions, quality failures, labor constraints, geopolitical tensions, or financial distress. A 30-day interruption could delay approximately 18,000 finished units and require premium freight, expedited qualification, or production rescheduling. Current inventory provides an estimated seven weeks of coverage, but that buffer may be insufficient during a prolonged disruption or a simultaneous demand increase.

Management should treat the concentration as a high-priority operational and compliance risk. By December 31, 2026, Procurement should qualify at least two additional suppliers: one in Vietnam or Malaysia and one in Mexico or the United States. The objective should be to reduce the Shenzhen vendor’s share below 40% by June 30, 2027, and below 25% by December 31, 2027. Engineering and Quality should complete second-source testing, including material composition, magnetic performance, reliability, and change-control documentation, before shifting volume.

Until alternate sources are approved, Finance should maintain a risk reserve for expedited logistics and substitute materials, while Legal and Compliance should review applicable U.S. export controls, forced-labor requirements, sanctions screening, and supply-chain disclosure obligations. Procurement should also seek a 90-day safety-stock commitment, business-continuity plans, and contractual notification requirements for production, ownership, or regulatory changes.

The Chief Operating Officer should receive a monthly dashboard covering supplier allocation, inventory weeks on hand, qualification milestones, open corrective actions, and country-of-origin exposure. This concentration is manageable, but only with defined reduction targets, executive oversight, and funded second-source development.
