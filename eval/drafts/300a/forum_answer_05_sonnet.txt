The oven (or better, a toaster oven / air fryer) beats the microwave every time — microwaving turns crust into rubbery cardboard because it just heats the moisture trapped in the breading.

Here's what actually works:

1. **Let the chicken come to room temp** for about 20-30 minutes before reheating. Cold-to-hot straight from the fridge cooks unevenly and you end up overshooting the outside to get the center warm.

2. **Preheat oven to 400°F (200°C).** Set a wire rack inside a sheet pan — this is the part people skip and it matters most. Air circulating underneath keeps the bottom from steaming and going soggy, which is exactly what happens if you lay the pieces directly on the pan.

3. **Bake bone-in pieces 15-20 minutes, boneless/tenders 10-12 minutes**, flipping once halfway through. You're aiming for an internal temp of 165°F (74°C) — check with an instant-read thermometer at the thickest part near the bone.

4. **Skip foil or covering it** — that traps steam and undoes the whole point.

If you have an air fryer, it's even faster and arguably crispier: 375°F (190°C) for 4-6 minutes, no flipping needed since it circulates air on all sides. I've done this with leftover KFC-style thighs and Popeye's tenders and both came out nearly as crisp as fresh.

One more trick if the crust still feels a little soft after reheating: pop it under the broiler for the last 60-90 seconds, watching closely so it doesn't burn. That extra blast of direct heat crisps up any spots the convection missed.

Avoid the microwave entirely unless you truly don't care about the crust — even 30 seconds will noticeably soften it.
