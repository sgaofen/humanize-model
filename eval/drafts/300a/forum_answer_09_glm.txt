Dense banana bread with a clean toothpick is a classic symptom of underbaking plus too much moisture, and the toothpick test is misleading here. Bananas make the batter extremely wet, and the center can stay gummy and compressed even after the starches set enough that nothing sticks to a skewer. Here's what I'd check:

1. **Your oven temperature is probably off.** Get an oven thermometer (they're about $8 at Target). My old GE ran 25°F cool, which meant the outside set long before the middle. Dense centers almost always trace back to this.

2. **Internal temp beats the toothpick.** Banana bread is done at 200–205°F in the center. I use a Thermapen; any instant-read thermometer works. Pull it at 200°F and it'll finish carryover cooking perfectly.

3. **You're probably using too much banana.** Most recipes want about 1 cup mashed (roughly 3 medium bananas, ~240g). If you're dumping in 5 large ones "because they're going bad," you've added maybe half a cup of extra liquid. Extra banana won't bake out — it stays as gumminess. Freeze the extras instead.

4. **Check your leavening.** Baking soda dies fast. If yours has been open since 2023, replace it. Test it in hot water: no vigorous fizz, toss it.

5. **Lower the temp, lengthen the bake.** Try 325°F instead of 350°F for 60–75 minutes in a 9x5 pan. Slower baking lets the center catch up to the edges. Tent the top with foil at 45 minutes if it's browning too fast.

6. **Don't overmix.** Mix just until the flour disappears. Gluten development makes it chewy and tight.

7. **Let it cool completely in the pan** (at least an hour). Slicing warm banana bread makes the middle seem dense and wetter than it actually is.

If I had to bet on one fix: buy the thermometer and bake to 200–205°F. That single change solved this exact problem for me after two years of dense loaves.
