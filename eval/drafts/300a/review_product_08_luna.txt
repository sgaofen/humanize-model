“Spider-Man: Across the Spider-Verse” is what happens when animation stops behaving like a container for a story and becomes the story’s nervous system. Every frame feels hand-painted, hacked together, or pulled from a different universe at 24 frames per second.

Miles Morales swinging through Brooklyn has a totally different visual rhythm from Gwen Stacy’s watercolor world, where colors bleed and emotions literally reshape the background. Then the film drops us into Mumbattan, a dazzling collision of neon, comic-book panels, Indian architecture, and kinetic train chases. The Spider Society headquarters is an entire museum of animation styles: hundreds of Spider-People, each moving with their own strange physics, silhouettes, and visual language.

I loved how often the movie lets images carry the emotion. Gwen’s bedroom changing around her during her argument with her father says more than a page of dialogue. Miguel O’Hara’s body stretches and snaps like an angry graphic-novel sketch. The Spot evolves from a goofy white-and-black blemish into something genuinely nightmarish, as if the film itself is being erased around him.

Released on June 2, 2023, this is a blockbuster with the visual ambition of experimental cinema. It’s maximalist, messy, gorgeous, and constantly inventing new ways to make movement feel meaningful. I could freeze almost any frame and hang it on a wall, but the real miracle is seeing those frames collide, mutate, and sprint toward the impossible.
