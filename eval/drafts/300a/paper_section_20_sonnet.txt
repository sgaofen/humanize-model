Intranasal oxytocin has been proposed to enhance interpersonal trust, but evidence from placebo-controlled trials remains mixed. We tested whether a single dose of intranasal oxytocin increases trusting behavior in an economic exchange game and whether this effect depends on prior social experience.

In a double-blind, placebo-controlled, between-subjects design, 180 healthy adults (94 women, 86 men; mean age 24.3 years, SD 4.1) recruited from a university community between March and November 2025 were randomly assigned to receive 24 IU of intranasal oxytocin (n=90) or placebo (n=90) 45 minutes before completing a multi-round trust game with an anonymous partner. Trust was operationalized as the proportion of an endowed sum (10 monetary units per round, 12 rounds) transferred to a counterpart, with returned amounts multiplied by 1.5. Participants also completed the Interpersonal Trust Scale and a betrayal-history questionnaire before dosing.

Participants receiving oxytocin transferred a significantly greater proportion of their endowment (M=61.4%, SD=14.2) than those receiving placebo (M=52.7%, SD=15.8), t(178)=4.02, p<.001, Cohen's d=0.60. This effect was moderated by betrayal history (interaction F(1,176)=9.87, p=.002): oxytocin increased transfers among participants reporting low prior betrayal exposure (Δ=12.9 percentage points, p<.001) but had no reliable effect among those reporting high betrayal exposure (Δ=1.8 points, p=.41). No significant effects emerged for reciprocity (amount returned by partners) or for self-reported dispositional trust scores, suggesting the manipulation altered behavioral risk-taking toward a specific partner rather than general trust attitudes.

These findings, collected at a single laboratory site, support a role for oxytocin in facilitating trust-related decisions under uncertainty, while indicating that this facilitation is constrained by individual social history. Replication with pharmacokinetic monitoring and diverse samples is warranted before clinical or applied extrapolation.
