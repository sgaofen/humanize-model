Limitations

Several limitations of our federated learning framework across the 12 participating hospitals warrant discussion. First, non-IID data distribution across sites remained a persistent challenge despite the use of FedProx (μ=0.01) in place of vanilla FedAvg. Class imbalance in pathology prevalence varied substantially by site — for instance, Site 7 (a pediatric center in Lyon) contributed fewer than 40 positive pneumothorax cases over the 14-month collection period, compared to over 2,100 at Site 3 (a level-1 trauma center in Houston), which likely contributed to the 6.8-point AUC gap we observed between the best- and worst-performing sites (0.91 vs. 0.843) on held-out local test sets. Second, scanner and acquisition heterogeneity was not fully addressed by our harmonization preprocessing; 5 of the 12 hospitals used GE Revolution CT scanners while the remainder used a mix of Siemens SOMATOM and Canon Aquilion systems, and residual domain shift likely persists in reconstruction kernel and slice thickness that our histogram-matching step could not fully correct. Third, our communication protocol assumed reliable connectivity and synchronous rounds every 48 hours across all 12 sites over the 9-month training period (January–September 2025); in practice, 3 sites experienced dropout during at least one round, requiring us to fall back on stale-client averaging, which may have introduced additional bias against sites with intermittent infrastructure. Fourth, although we did not share raw imaging data, gradient updates were exchanged without formal differential privacy guarantees, leaving potential exposure to membership-inference or gradient-inversion attacks — a concern flagged by two of our participating IRBs (Houston and Lyon) but not fully resolved within this study's scope. Finally, our evaluation relied on site-specific held-out test sets rather than a fully external cohort, so generalization to hospitals outside this 12-site consortium remains unverified. Future work should incorporate personalized federated learning (e.g., per-cluster or per-site adapters) and formal privacy accounting to address these gaps before clinical deployment is considered.
