I clocked out of my last shift at St. Luke's Medical Center on a Tuesday in July 2024 and started a UX bootcamp the following Monday. Fourteen months later, I'm three weeks into a product designer role at a healthtech startup in Providence, and I want to write down how it actually happened, because the timeline I imagined at the start was nothing like the one I lived.

Months 1-3 were General Assembly's part-time UX/UI course, done on top of three 12-hour night shifts a week at the hospital. I'd finish a shift at 7am, sleep until 1pm, and log on for the 3pm cohort call. I completed two projects in that window: a redesign of my hospital's own patient discharge instructions (genuinely terrible paperwork, it turns out, once you look at it as a designer instead of a nurse handing it out) and a medication reminder app called Dosewise.

Months 4-7 were the hard middle. I quit nursing entirely in November 2024 — we'd saved eight months of expenses specifically for this — and spent those months doing informational interviews. I did 34 of them, tracked in a spreadsheet, mostly found through a Providence UX meetup called Ocean State UX that meets the second Thursday of every month at a coworking space on Westminster Street. Eleven of those calls turned into portfolio feedback sessions.

Months 8-10 I built a third case study, this one unsolicited: a redesign of the nurse shift-handoff tool at my old hospital, interviewing six former coworkers about where information gets lost between shifts. That project got more callbacks than the other two combined, because it was the one where my nursing background was the whole point instead of something I had to explain away.

Months 11-14: 61 applications, 9 first-round interviews, 3 final rounds, 1 offer. The offer came from Verve Health, a 40-person company building tools for home health aides, on September 2, 2025. My starting salary is $89,000 — about $6,000 less than my last nursing year with differentials, but with no more nights.

If someone's starting this now: budget for month 5 through 7 to feel like nothing is happening. It's not nothing. It's the interviews compounding.
