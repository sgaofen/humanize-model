One year ago this week, I walked into Cleveland Clinic's Family Health Center in Independence as a brand-new nurse practitioner, badge still squeaky clean, heart pounding. Today, that badge is scuffed, my coffee is cold more often than not, and I wouldn't trade a single day.

This flu season tested everything I learned at Case Western's MSN program. Between October and February, our team administered over 3,200 flu vaccines and managed countless positive cases. I'll never forget a January Tuesday when I diagnosed eleven cases of influenza A before lunch — including a 68-year-old patient named Margaret whose symptoms I almost attributed to "just a bad cold." Her rapid test reminded me why we never assume.

Three things this year taught me:
1️⃣ Listening is diagnostic. Some of my best clinical catches came from letting patients talk for 60 uninterrupted seconds.
2️⃣ Ask for help early. My collaborating physician, Dr. Patel, has seen it all — and taught me that questions are a strength, not a weakness.
3️⃣ Celebrate the small wins. A patient telling me "that's the first time a provider explained this to me" matters as much as any lab result.

To my preceptors, my triage nurses who kept me sane, and every patient who trusted a new NP with their care: thank you.

Year two, I'm ready for you. Flu season 2025-26, bring it on. 💉🩺
