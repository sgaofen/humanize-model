**Methods**

Participants were 240 veterans (mean age 47.3 years, SD = 12.1; 68% male) recruited between March 2021 and November 2022 from outpatient clinics at the VA Puget Sound Health Care System (Seattle, WA) and the VA Boston Healthcare System (Boston, MA). Eligibility required a DSM-5 diagnosis of chronic insomnia disorder, Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) score ≥15, and no active suicidal ideation or untreated psychosis. Veterans with comorbid PTSD (n = 142, 59%) were retained to reflect the target population. After screening 412 referrals, 240 were randomized 1:1 using a computer-generated permuted-block schedule (block size 4, stratified by site and PTSD status) to cognitive-behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I; n = 120) or an active control, sleep hygiene education plus attention-matched contact (n = 120).

CBT-I was delivered in six weekly 50-minute individual sessions by 14 doctoral-level psychologists trained to a fidelity criterion of ≥90% on a standardized checklist, and included sleep restriction, stimulus control, cognitive restructuring, and relapse prevention. Control-arm participants received one 30-minute sleep hygiene session followed by five weekly 20-minute check-in calls. Assessments occurred at baseline, post-treatment (week 7), and 6-month follow-up (through May 2023). The primary outcome was ISI score; secondary outcomes included sleep diary–derived sleep efficiency, actigraphy (Actiwatch-2) over 7 consecutive nights, PTSD Checklist for DSM-5 (PCL-5) scores, and Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) scores. Outcome assessors were blinded to condition. Sample size (n = 120/arm) was powered at 0.90 to detect a between-group difference of 4 points on the ISI (SD = 6.5), assuming 15% attrition, alpha = .05, two-tailed. Primary analyses used intention-to-treat linear mixed-effects models with random intercepts for participant and site, adjusting for baseline ISI and PTSD status. The protocol was approved by the VA Central Institutional Review Board (IRB #2020-0847) and registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04739228). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to randomization.
