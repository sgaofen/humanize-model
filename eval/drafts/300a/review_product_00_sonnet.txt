Six months in, my iPhone 15 Pro is still the best phone I've owned, with two clear caveats.

Battery life is good, not great. On a typical day—maybe 4 hours of screen time mixing Slack, Spotify, and camera use—I land around 9pm with 20-25% left, starting from a 7am unplug. Heavier days (navigation on a road trip to Lake Tahoe in March, or shooting video at a friend's wedding in June) can drain it to single digits by early evening. Apple's 80% health threshold after 500 cycles feels honest: Settings still shows 91% capacity, and I haven't noticed real degradation yet. I charge overnight on a 20W USB-C brick and top up with MagSafe at my desk; wireless charging is noticeably slower and warmer, so I avoid it unless I'm in a hurry.

The camera is the real upgrade from my old 13 Pro. The 48MP main sensor produces genuinely printable shots—I've blown a few up to 11x14 for a gallery wall with no visible softness. The 3x telephoto (77mm equivalent) is the one I use most, especially for candid shots at restaurants where getting closer would be awkward. Low light has improved too; a dim bar in downtown Austin in April produced usable, not-too-noisy photos handheld. My one gripe: Night mode still hunts for focus for 2-3 seconds longer than I'd like, and portrait mode occasionally blurs earring details it shouldn't.

At $999 for the 128GB model, it's expensive, but between the titanium build surviving a couple of drops unscathed and the camera replacing what used to be a separate point-and-shoot for me, I'd buy it again.
