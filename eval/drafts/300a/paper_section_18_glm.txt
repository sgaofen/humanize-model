**Results**

The ensemble of five ResNet-50 architectures achieved a mean classification accuracy of 91.4% on the held-out test partition of the ISIC 2020 dataset (n = 3,326 dermoscopic images), surpassing all individual baseline models. The best single model reached 89.1% accuracy, while the weakest attained 86.7%, indicating that ensemble averaging yielded a 2.3 percentage-point gain over the strongest standalone network. Sensitivity for malignant melanoma detection was 88.9% (95% CI: 86.4–91.1%), with specificity of 92.6% (95% CI: 90.8–94.2%). The ensemble's area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) was 0.941, compared with a range of 0.908–0.927 across individual models.

Misclassification analysis revealed that 61.3% of false negatives occurred in images classified by two board-certified dermatologists (blinded review, Cohen's κ = 0.71) as ambiguous or low-contrast lesions, particularly acral melanomas and heavily pigmented nevi with regression structures. False positives were concentrated among seborrheic keratoses with inflammatory halos and recurrent nevi, consistent with known pitfalls in dermoscopic interpretation.

Class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) showed that the ensemble attended to pigment network structures and atypical streaking in 78.4% of correctly classified malignant cases, whereas single models more frequently fixated on image artifacts such as ruler markings and gel bubbles (12.1% of Grad-CAM heatmaps, vs. 4.6% for the ensemble), suggesting that aggregation attenuated spurious feature reliance.

Subgroup performance stratified by Fitzpatrick skin type showed no statistically significant decline for types IV–VI (accuracy 90.2%, p = 0.18, DeLong test), though the modest sample size (n = 281) limits power. Training with 5-fold cross-validation on the 26,604-image training split required approximately 14 hours per fold on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU; total training and inference time was 96 hours. The ensemble's performance approached but did not exceed the 92.8% accuracy of the 76-dermatologist consensus panel evaluated on the identical test set (n = 3,326, McNemar's test p = 0.06). These results indicate that ResNet-50 ensembling provides a robust and dermatologist-comparable baseline for melanoma triage, while highlighting residual vulnerability to diagnostically ambiguous lesion morphologies.
