## Discussion

The 8-week high-fat diet (HFD; 60% kcal from fat) induced pronounced and largely reproducible shifts in the murine cecal microbiome at the genus level, consistent with prior reports (Bäckhed et al., 2004; Turnbaugh et al., 2006), while adding quantitative resolution on effect sizes and inter-individual variability. In our C57BL/6J cohort (n = 12 HFD, n = 11 chow-fed controls), the most robust signal was a 14.3-fold expansion of *Bilophila* (log2 fold change = 3.84; adjusted q = 0.002, DESeq2 with Benjamini–Hochberg correction), an organism whose bile-tolerant physiology aligns mechanistically with the elevated tauroconjugated bile acids that accompany dietary fat loads. This finding corroborates the proposed link between *Bilophila*-derived sulfide production and intestinal barrier impairment.

Conversely, we observed a marked depletion of *Akkermansia* (log2FC = −2.61; q = 0.008), a mucin-degrading genus repeatedly associated with metabolic health in both rodents and humans. Whether *Akkermansia* loss is a driver or a passenger of HFD-induced metabolic endotoxemia remains unresolved; our data show that its decline preceded measurable fasting glucose elevation by roughly two weeks, hinting at a directional relationship, though the present design cannot establish causality.

Two findings warrant cautious interpretation. First, the *Lactobacillus* decline (log2FC = −1.78; q = 0.03) was driven largely by three animals, and sensitivity analysis excluding these outliers rendered the effect non-significant (q = 0.11). Second, the apparent bloom of *Clostridium* XIVa (log2FC = 1.92; q = 0.048) sat at the edge of significance and may reflect spillover from compositional normalization rather than true abundance change, a limitation inherent to relative-count data that we partially addressed with ANCOM-BC as a confirmatory method.

Alpha diversity declined modestly in HFD animals (mean Shannon index 3.12 vs. 3.58; Wilcoxon p = 0.009), and beta diversity separated groups along PCo1 (PERMANOVA R² = 0.19; p = 0.001), indicating that the dietary perturbation restructured the community beyond single-taxon effects. Whether these shifts are reversible upon diet normalization, and which genera track recovery of insulin sensitivity, are the subject of ongoing follow-up in our laboratory.
