1/3 Cold-emailing 200 VCs for my seed round in spring 2024 taught me 5 painful lessons.

Mistake #1: I sent the same generic pitch to everyone. “Hi, we’re building the future of collaboration” meant nothing to a fintech investor in New York or a healthcare investor in Boston.

Mistake #2: I led with product features instead of traction. Our strongest proof was $18,400 MRR and 37% month-over-month growth in March. I buried it on slide 12.

2/3 Mistake #3: I emailed partners who never invest at the seed stage. Several managed $500M+ funds but wrote their first checks at Series A. I should have read their websites and recent deals before reaching out.

Mistake #4: I asked for “15 minutes to connect” instead of making a clear, specific ask: “Would you consider leading a $1.5M seed round for a B2B workflow tool growing 37% MoM?”

3/3 Mistake #5: I followed up emotionally and inconsistently. Some VCs got four emails in 10 days; others got none after the first message. My eventual system was simple: 3 thoughtful follow-ups over 21 days, each with a new data point.

We closed the round in July 2024. The biggest lesson: cold email is not a numbers game alone. Relevance, proof, and timing matter more than sending 200 messages.
