In F. Scott Fitzgerald’s 1925 novel *The Great Gatsby*, the green light at the end of Daisy Buchanan’s dock symbolizes Jay Gatsby’s hopes, especially his desire to reunite with Daisy and recreate their past relationship. Gatsby lives in West Egg, across the bay from Daisy’s home in East Egg, and he often reaches toward the small green light shining from her dock. Although the light is physically close enough to see, the water between Gatsby and Daisy represents the emotional, social, and historical distance separating them. Gatsby believes that wealth can overcome these barriers, so he builds a mansion, hosts extravagant parties, and becomes rich in order to impress Daisy. However, the green light also symbolizes the American Dream because Gatsby imagines that happiness is always just beyond his reach. Like many people who pursue success, he believes that achieving one more goal will finally make his life complete. The light’s green color suggests hope, renewal, and desire, but it can also suggest money, connecting Gatsby’s dream of Daisy to his belief that wealth can purchase love and status. Nick Carraway eventually realizes that Gatsby’s dream is based on an idealized memory rather than reality. Gatsby does not simply want Daisy as she is in 1922; he wants to return to the romance they shared before he left for World War I and before Daisy married Tom. His famous insistence that the past can be repeated reveals how completely he confuses hope with possibility. By the novel’s ending, Gatsby is dead, Daisy has abandoned him, and the green light continues to shine across the bay. Its meaning expands beyond Gatsby’s personal dream to represent the nation’s endless pursuit of an imagined future. Fitzgerald’s final image of people struggling forward while being pushed backward suggests that the green light is both inspiring and tragic: it gives individuals something to pursue, yet it may also prevent them from accepting reality.
