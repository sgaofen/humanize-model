For a freelance designer, I’d aim for 6–12 months of essential personal expenses, not an arbitrary dollar amount. Irregular income makes the upper end more appropriate than the usual three-month target.

For example, if you live in Portland, Oregon, and your bare-bones monthly costs are:

- Rent and utilities: $1,800  
- Health insurance and medical costs: $500  
- Food: $500  
- Transportation: $250  
- Minimum debt payments and other necessities: $150  

Your essential budget is $3,200 per month. A reasonable emergency-fund target would therefore be $19,200 for six months, with $28,800 providing a stronger 9-month cushion.

Keep separate accounts for three different purposes:

1. Emergency fund: job gaps, illness, or major unexpected expenses.
2. Tax reserve: money for quarterly federal and state estimated taxes.
3. Business reserve: software, equipment replacement, subcontractors, and slow client payments.

Otherwise, a large tax bill or broken laptop can make your “emergency fund” look healthier than it really is.

I’d calculate the target using your lowest realistic monthly spending, not your average lifestyle. Pay yourself a consistent amount—say $4,000 monthly—from your business account, and send unusually strong months into savings. Refill the fund after using it, and keep it in an easily accessible FDIC-insured savings account rather than investments.

The CFPB specifically identifies emergency funds as useful for people with irregular income, while the FDIC cites three to six months as a general guideline and notes that people with especially uncertain employment may need six months or more. [CFPB guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/) and [FDIC guidance](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/2025-01/saving-unexpected-and-your-future).
