**Field Research Log: Bird Population Counts — Cedar Swamp**
**Week of May 6–15, 2024**
**Submitted by: Dana Whitfield, Field Technician, Northline Audubon Society**

Summary of Activity

Over ten consecutive survey days, our three-person team (D. Whitfield, M. Okafor, and intern J. Reyes) completed 40 point-count surveys across the Cedar Swamp monitoring grid, covering all eight established stations in both morning (0530–0900) and evening (1700–1930) windows. Weather was generally cooperative; only the May 9 morning count was curtailed by heavy rain.

Results

We recorded a total of 42 species, up from 37 during the same period last year. Total individual detections numbered 1,184. Highlights and notables:

- **Virginia rail** — detected at Station 3 on four occasions, suggesting a possible breeding pair; last confirmed in 2019.
- **Prothonotary warbler** — single male singing at Station 6 on May 11 and May 13; flagged for follow-up nest-box evaluation.
- **Great blue heron** — active rookery near the north dike with at least 6 occupied nests.
- **Swamp sparrow and common yellowthroat** remained the most abundant species, accounting for roughly 22% of all detections.

Abundance by station was uneven: Stations 3 and 6 accounted for 38% of total detections, likely reflecting the expanded emergent marsh habitat following last fall's hydrology restoration.

Data entry is complete in eBird and the internal Northline database. Audio recordings from the autonomous recording units (ARUs) at Stations 2 and 7 still need review; J. Reyes will process those by May 22.

Issues and Recommendations

1. The boardwalk between Stations 4 and 5 is deteriorating; two planks are loose. Recommend maintenance before the June survey round.
2. Mosquito populations are high this season — teams should carry repellent for remaining spring counts.
3. Given the prothonotary warbler detection, recommend deploying two nest boxes near Station 6 before June 1.

Next Steps

The second survey round runs June 3–12. Team assignments will be circulated by May 27. Please direct questions to me at d.whitfield@northlineaudubon.org.
