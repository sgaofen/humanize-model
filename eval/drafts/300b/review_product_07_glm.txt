I just wrapped up a 3,000-mile loop from Portland down to San Diego and back in my 2022 Toyota RAV4 Hybrid, and I'm honestly impressed enough to write this up.

The headline number: 39.8 mpg average over the whole trip. That's hand-calculated across nine fill-ups, not just the dash readout (which actually ran about 1 mpg optimistic). Toyota rates this thing at 40 mpg combined, and on flat highway stretches through the Central Valley I regularly saw 42-43 mpg sitting at 70 mph. Mountain passes near Grant's Pass and the Grapevine dragged it down to around 34, but that's expected.

The 2.5-liter hybrid system is smooth, if not thrilling. Merging onto I-5 requires some planning — the 219 hp feels adequate rather than eager, and the CVT drones when you floor it. But that's not what this car is for. What surprised me was the comfort: after 500-mile days, my back and my wife's both survived fine. The seats are supportive, road noise is acceptable (noticeably quieter than the gas-only RAV4 I test-drove in 2021), and the cargo area swallowed two duffels, a cooler, and a dog crate with room left over.

A few gripes. The infotainment screen looks a generation behind, and the lane-keeping assist on the XLE trim nags constantly on winding Highway 101 — we ended up just switching it off. Also, the brakes feel a bit grabby in the first few inches of pedal travel, which is common in Toyota hybrids but takes a couple hundred miles to get used to.

Fuel math for the trip: about 75 gallons total, roughly $340 at California and Oregon prices. A comparable gas SUV would've cost nearly double that.

Would I buy it again? Absolutely. For a road-trip vehicle that sips fuel, hauls gear, and asks for nothing but oil changes, the RAV4 Hybrid is hard to beat.
