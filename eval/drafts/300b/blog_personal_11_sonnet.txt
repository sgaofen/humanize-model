I hit send on my first invoice on October 14th last year — $340, for a blog post about industrial water filtration, a topic I knew nothing about three days earlier. I've now sent 118 more invoices. Total for the year: $38,240, across 27 clients, and I want to write down what actually happened before I round it off into a tidy story.

The number that surprised me wasn't the total, it was the distribution: four clients account for $24,000 of it. A SaaS company called Ledgerly pays me $1,400 a month for a newsletter and two blog posts. A dental marketing agency in Ohio sends me $600 a week like clockwork. The other 23 clients — one-off landing pages, email sequences, a $75 product description for a dog leash company — added up to the remaining $14,000 and took up probably 60% of my hours. I didn't understand fixed costs versus retainers until I graphed it in July.

Mistake one: I quoted a fintech client a flat $2,200 for "website copy" without defining pages. Eleven pages later, in March, I was effectively making $9 an hour. I finished it because I'd already said yes.

Mistake two: I didn't set aside tax money until my accountant, Priya, asked in April if I'd made estimated payments. I hadn't. I owed $6,100 by June 15th and had to pull from savings.

Mistake three: I kept a client, a supplement brand, for five months after their checks started arriving 20-30 days late, because I liked the work. By the time I fired them in August, they owed me $1,900, which I never fully collected.

None of that means it wasn't worth it. I left a $61,000 salary job in September of last year, so $38,240 while learning everything above, in one year, from zero, is a number I'll take. Year two goal: fewer clients, better contracts, and a kill switch for late payers at 15 days, not five months.
