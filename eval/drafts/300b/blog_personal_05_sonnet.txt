Landed at Kansai on a Tuesday in October with ¥15,000 (about $100) a day as the ceiling, and left convinced Kyoto is one of the easier cities to do this in — you're just trading taxis for a bicycle and Michelin stars for tofu shops.

**Day one.** Haikyu Hostel near Kyoto Station, ¥3,400 a night for a dorm bed, ICOCA card loaded at the station kiosk. Rented a bike from the hostel for ¥800/day instead of buses — Kyoto is flat and bike lanes are everywhere. Breakfast: a pastry and coffee from a Lawson konbini, ¥450. Rode to Fushimi Inari before 8am, before the tour buses; the gates are free and you can walk up to the Yotsutsuji overlook in about 40 minutes. Lunch at a standing soba counter near the shrine, ¥600 for kitsune soba. Afternoon at Tofuku-ji (¥500 entry) for the garden, then biked to Gion and just walked — no cost to wander Hanamikoji Street or peek into Yasaka Shrine, which is free. Dinner: a bowl of ramen at a counter spot in Pontocho, ¥900, plus a can of Asahi from a vending machine, ¥150. Day total: about ¥10,200, under budget.

**Day two.** Skipped the hostel breakfast and grabbed onigiri from a konbini, ¥300. Biked out to Arashiyama for the bamboo grove — free, best before 9am when it's just you and the creak of the stalks. Tenryu-ji temple next door is ¥500 and worth it for the pond garden. Crossed Togetsukyo Bridge, then a cheap curry lunch near the station, ¥750. Spent the early afternoon at Nishiki Market grazing instead of eating a sit-down meal — a skewer here, a piece of tamagoyaki there, maybe ¥1,200 total for enough food to count as lunch and a snack. Kiyomizu-dera in late afternoon light, ¥400 entry, then walked down through Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka, which cost nothing but are the best-looking streets in the city. Last dinner: katsu curry, ¥1,100.

Two days, two nights, roughly ¥19,000 total — meaning I banked almost a full extra day's budget by day two. The trick isn't deprivation, it's timing temples before the crowds (which also means before you're tempted to buy overpriced tea to escape them) and letting a bike replace half your transit budget.
