As of September 21, 2026, the proposed $340 million acquisition of TerraFusion Energy’s geothermal assets merits approval, subject to environmental, title, and production due diligence. The portfolio includes the 48-MW Silver Basin project near Winnemucca, Nevada; the 32-MW Coso Ridge expansion in Inyo County, California; and associated exploration leases covering approximately 41,000 acres across Nevada and eastern California.

The offer implies an estimated $4.25 million per installed megawatt for the 80 MW currently in operation or late-stage development. That valuation is competitive for firm, renewable generation, particularly given the assets’ potential eligibility for federal clean-energy tax credits and their ability to provide 24-hour capacity. Preliminary estimates indicate that the portfolio could generate approximately $58 million in annual revenue at an average contracted power price of $72 per megawatt-hour, before operating costs and royalties.

The principal risks are resource depletion, permitting delays, water-use restrictions, and TerraFusion’s outstanding obligations to county authorities and tribal stakeholders. The transaction should therefore be structured with $290 million payable at closing and up to $50 million in earn-out consideration tied to production milestones, permitting outcomes, and successful commissioning of the Coso Ridge expansion by December 31, 2028.

Before signing, the acquiring company should require an independent reservoir assessment, a review of all 37 federal and state permits, confirmation of land and mineral rights, and a five-year maintenance and capital plan. Management should also secure power-purchase agreements covering at least 70% of expected output and maintain a reserve of no less than $35 million for well remediation and unplanned drilling.

With these protections, the acquisition would strengthen the company’s western U.S. renewable-generation platform, add dependable baseload capacity, and support its 2030 emissions-reduction strategy. Without milestone-based pricing and a verified resource assessment, the $340 million headline valuation should not be accepted.
