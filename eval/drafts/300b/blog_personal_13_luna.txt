In January 2025, my A1C was 8.4%, and I was tired of pretending that “eating pretty well” was a measurable plan. My doctor, Dr. Lena Morales at Kaiser Permanente in Sacramento, said medication was an option, but because I had no urgent symptoms, we agreed to try six months of carefully tracked food, walking, sleep, and weight loss first.

I bought a cheap spiral notebook and started writing down everything—not just meals, but times, portions, and how I felt afterward. My first breakfast journal entry read: “7:30 a.m.—bagel with cream cheese, banana, coffee.” Two hours later, my glucose was 196 mg/dL. I didn’t panic, but I did notice a pattern. Toast, rice, cereal, and large portions of potatoes pushed my numbers up quickly.

By February, I was making eggs with spinach, plain Greek yogurt with walnuts, or leftover chicken and vegetables. I still ate tacos on Fridays at El Rincon on Fruitridge Road, but I had two instead of four and skipped the sugary horchata. After dinner, my wife, Marisol, and I walked around our neighborhood for 20 minutes. Eventually, those walks became 35 minutes, five nights a week.

The hardest part was not perfection—it was consistency. At my grandson Mateo’s birthday in April, I ate a slice of chocolate cake. I wrote it down instead of calling the day ruined. My journal helped me see that one dessert was not the problem; giving up for three weeks afterward was.

At my June appointment, my A1C had fallen to 7.1%. By September, it was 6.5%, and I was down 24 pounds from January. My latest test, on January 10, 2026, showed 6.1%. I have managed this year without diabetes medication, but only with regular appointments and my doctor’s approval. I still check my glucose, and I know my plan could change.

The notebooks are now stacked beside my coffee maker. They contain plenty of imperfect days, but also proof that small decisions—less rice, more vegetables, a walk after dinner—can add up to a completely different year.
