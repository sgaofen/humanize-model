1/ I shut down my first startup, **RelayCart**, in November 2021 after 18 months of work. We raised $120,000, reached 47 paying customers, and still couldn’t make the business sustainable.

2/ Lesson 1: A problem can be real without being urgent. Small retailers liked our inventory dashboard, but few were willing to change their routines—or pay for it.

3/ Lesson 2: Revenue is the only validation that compounds. Praise, waitlists, and “I’d definitely use this” comments didn’t cover payroll.

4/ Lesson 3: We hired too early. By March 2021, our four-person team was burning $14,000/month before product-market fit.

5/ Lesson 4: Talk to customers every week. I spent too much time building in Oakland and not enough time visiting the store owners we claimed to serve.

6/ Lesson 5: Co-founder alignment matters. My co-founder wanted to pursue fundraising; I wanted to focus on profitability. We avoided that conflict until it became impossible to ignore.

7/ Lesson 6: Set kill criteria. We should have shut down after six months below $5,000 MRR, not after the bank account hit $8,300.

8/ Lesson 7: Failure is expensive tuition—but only if you study it. I’m now building slower, selling earlier, and measuring behavior instead of enthusiasm.
