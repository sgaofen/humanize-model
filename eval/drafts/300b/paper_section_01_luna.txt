AlphaFold2, announced after the 2020 CASP14 assessment and published in *Nature* in 2021, marked a decisive shift in protein structure prediction. Its Evoformer repeatedly exchanged information between multiple-sequence-alignment (MSA) and residue-pair representations through attention and outer-product updates, while a structure module based on invariant point attention (IPA) converted these representations into three-dimensional coordinates. By combining sequence evolution with geometric reasoning and recycling intermediate predictions, AlphaFold2 achieved a median GDT-TS score of 92.4 in CASP14, including a median score of 87.0 on difficult free-modeling targets. IPA was particularly influential because its attention operates on learned three-dimensional points while remaining invariant to global rotations and translations. ([Jumper et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03819-2))

Subsequent architectures diversified this attention-centered design. RoseTTAFold, developed at the University of Washington and reported in *Science* in 2021, used a three-track network that simultaneously processed one-dimensional sequence/MSA features, two-dimensional residue-pair information, and three-dimensional coordinates. Attention, graph-transformer operations, and SE(3)-equivariant layers allowed information to circulate among these representations; for proteins shorter than 400 residues, its end-to-end implementation generated backbone coordinates in approximately 10 minutes on an RTX 2080 GPU. ([Baek et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj8754)) In parallel, protein language models reframed structure prediction as a consequence of sequence-scale representation learning. Meta’s ESM-2 and ESMFold used Transformer language-model embeddings to predict structures from single sequences, avoiding the computational cost of explicit MSAs. Published in *Science* in 2023, ESMFold enabled the ESM Metagenomic Atlas, containing predictions for more than 617 million proteins, although its accuracy generally remained below AlphaFold2’s. ([Lin et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ade2574))

AlphaFold3, published in *Nature* in 2024, extended this trajectory from protein folding toward biomolecular interaction modeling. It replaced AlphaFold2’s IPA-based structure module with a diffusion-based generative architecture capable of jointly modeling proteins, nucleic acids, ligands, ions, and modified residues. Thus, transformer-based protein prediction has evolved from MSA-conditioned geometric attention toward multimodal foundation models that combine language representations, equivariant geometry, and diffusion-based coordinate generation.
