⭐⭐⭐⭐ Trattoria Roma — Carbonara Worth the Trip

Stopped into Trattoria Roma on a rainy Friday night (March 14th) around 7:30 with my sister after a Cubs game. We were quoted a 25-minute wait but got seated in closer to 15, which was a nice surprise for a Friday. The space is cozy—exposed brick, warm lighting, a little noisy but in a lively way, not a "can't hear each other" way.

Let's talk about the carbonara, because that's why I'm writing this. At $19.95, it's priced right in line with the neighborhood, but the execution punched way above that. The pasta was clearly made in-house—you can tell by the texture—and cooked just past al dente, which I know some purists will clutch pearls at, but it worked. The sauce was properly silky: no cream in sight, just egg yolk, pecorino, and that rendered guanciale goodness. The pork itself was cut into generous, crispy-edged chunks, not the sad little bacon bits you get at lesser places. A healthy grind of black pepper tied it together with real bite. Portion size was substantial; I couldn't finish it, and I rarely say that.

Service was attentive without hovering. Our server, Daniela, checked in at the right moments and kept the water glasses full. Bread basket came with a herbed olive oil dip that we demolished while waiting.

Minor gripes: the house Chianti ($9/glass) was a bit thin, and the table wobbled until Daniela folded a napkin under the leg. Also, street parking in this part of town is a nightmare—spring for the valet.

Total for two, with a shared tiramisu (excellent, by the way—real espresso punch) and drinks, came to $78 before tip. Fair value for the quality. I'll be back to try the cacio e pepe.
