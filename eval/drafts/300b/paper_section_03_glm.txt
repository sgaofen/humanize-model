**Results**

Cesium bromide (CsBr) incorporation into the methylammonium lead iodide (MAPbI₃) perovskite precursor solution produced a statistically significant improvement in power conversion efficiency (PCE). Champion devices fabricated with 5 mol% CsBr achieved a PCE of 19.4%, compared with 18.1% for undoped control devices, corresponding to a relative yield improvement of 7.2% (absolute gain of 1.3 percentage points). This enhancement was reproduced across three independent fabrication batches prepared between March and May 2024, with champion-device PCEs of 19.1–19.6% for CsBr-doped cells versus 17.8–18.3% for controls.

Current density–voltage (J–V) measurements under AM 1.5G illumination (100 mW cm⁻²) showed that the efficiency gain was driven primarily by increased open-circuit voltage (V_OC): 1.12 ± 0.02 V for doped devices versus 1.06 ± 0.03 V for controls (n = 24 devices per condition, p < 0.001, two-tailed t-test). Short-circuit current density (J_SC) improved modestly from 22.4 ± 0.6 to 22.9 ± 0.5 mA cm⁻², while fill factor remained statistically unchanged (FF = 0.74 ± 0.02 both groups). Hysteresis indices decreased from 8.3% to 4.1% with CsBr addition, consistent with reduced ion migration.

X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis of the doped films revealed a 0.14° shift of the (110) reflection toward higher 2θ and suppression of the PbI₂ peak at 12.7°, indicating partial substitution of Cs⁺ into the A-site and improved phase purity. Scanning electron microscopy showed enlarged grains (mean grain size 412 ± 95 nm vs. 287 ± 78 nm for controls), which correlated with a reduction in trap-state density from 1.8 × 10¹⁶ to 1.1 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ as measured by space-charge-limited current analysis.

Stability testing under continuous illumination at 65 °C showed doped devices retaining 88% of initial PCE after 500 hours, versus 74% for controls. Doping concentrations above 5 mol% (tested at 10 and 15 mol%) led to phase segregation and diminished performance (PCE 17.2% and 15.9%, respectively), establishing 5 mol% CsBr as optimal within the studied compositional window.
