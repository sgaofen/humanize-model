For a 2-inch hole, use a self-adhesive metal drywall patch rather than trying to fill the opening with joint compound alone.

First, trim away loose paper and square up any ragged edges with a utility knife. Wipe off dust. Apply a 4-inch or larger metal mesh patch centered over the hole; press it firmly so it lies flat. If the damage is near a corner or stud, make sure the patch does not bridge an uneven surface.

Cover the patch with setting-type joint compound, such as 45-minute Easy Sand. Use a 6-inch taping knife and feather the compound at least 3 inches beyond the patch. Let it harden, then scrape off ridges and apply a second, thinner coat. A third skim coat may be needed to hide the patch completely.

After the final coat dries—usually overnight—sand lightly with 150- or 220-grit sandpaper. Run your hand over the area; if you can feel an edge, apply more compound rather than sanding aggressively. Wipe away dust, prime the repair with drywall primer, and paint with the matching wall color.

Because a door knob caused the hole, install a doorstop or hinge pin stop after repairing it. For a knob that hits the wall, a wall-mounted rubber stop is usually best. If the hole is close to an electrical outlet or switch, turn off the circuit before cutting or sanding and avoid driving screws in that area.
