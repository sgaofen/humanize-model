We conducted a field experiment from October 2023 to August 2024 among 1,150 smallholder farmers cultivating vegetables in Kenya’s Makueni and Kitui counties. Households were identified through village-level agricultural registers and screened for eligibility: respondents had to cultivate between 0.1 and 2.0 hectares, rely primarily on rain-fed or manually irrigated production, and not currently use drip irrigation. After baseline surveys, farmers were stratified by county and baseline irrigated area and randomly assigned to one of three groups: information only (n = 383), information plus a 50% voucher for a drip-irrigation kit (n = 384), or information plus a 75% voucher (n = 383).

All treatment households attended a two-hour demonstration conducted by locally trained agricultural extension agents in November 2023. The sessions explained installation, maintenance, expected water savings, and crop-specific application rates using a 0.1-hectare demonstration plot. Vouchers were redeemable through participating agro-dealers in Makueni and Kitui until February 29, 2024. The kits included a 500-liter storage tank, a low-pressure filter, 100 meters of lateral tubing, connectors, and an estimated capacity to irrigate 0.1 hectare. Farmers in the information-only group received the same technical information but no price subsidy.

Enumerators who were blinded to treatment assignment conducted in-person surveys at baseline, four months after voucher expiration, and at the end of the 2024 main growing season. The primary outcome was adoption, defined as installation and use of a drip system on at least 0.05 hectares during the 30 days preceding the endline survey. Secondary outcomes included cultivated area, irrigation frequency, input expenditures, vegetable yields, farm revenue, water use, and reported labor requirements. Crop production was measured using farmer records and randomly selected crop-cutting plots. We estimated intention-to-treat effects using ordinary least squares, controlling for county fixed effects and pre-specified baseline covariates, with standard errors clustered at the village level.
