Good news — 120/80 at age 55 is a solidly healthy number. In fact, that's essentially the textbook ideal.

For context, the American Heart Association considers anything under 120/80 to be "normal," and 120–129 systolic with diastolic under 80 to be "elevated." Stage 1 hypertension starts at 130/80, and Stage 2 at 140/90. So you're right at the top of the normal range, which is exactly where many healthy 55-year-olds land. Blood pressure does tend to creep up with age as blood vessels stiffen, so holding at 120/80 in your mid-fifties is a good sign, not a warning.

A few caveats worth mentioning:

1. **One reading isn't a diagnosis.** Blood pressure varies throughout the day and with stress, caffeine, sleep, even talking during the measurement. If that 120/80 came from a single reading at a pharmacy machine or during a stressful doctor's visit, take a few more readings at home — seated, arm supported, rested for 5 minutes — over a week or two and see what your average looks like.

2. **Ask about your 10-year risk profile, not just the number.** At 55, your doctor should also be looking at cholesterol, blood glucose, family history, smoking, and weight. Someone can have fine blood pressure but elevated risk from other factors.

3. **Keep monitoring it.** Guidelines suggest checking at least annually at your physical, or more often if you have a family history of hypertension.

The usual advice applies for keeping it where it is: moderate sodium (under 2,300 mg/day, ideally closer to 1,500), regular aerobic exercise (150 minutes a week), limit alcohol, and watch your weight.

Bottom line: no, this isn't a concern — it's a number most 55-year-olds would be happy to see. But do mention it at your next checkup and keep an eye on the trend over time, since that matters more than any single reading.

*(Standard disclaimer: I'm not a doctor — if you have symptoms like headaches, dizziness, or chest discomfort, see your physician regardless of the numbers.)*
