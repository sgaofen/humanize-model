On March 8, 2023, I opened my laptop at 7:45 a.m. Pacific and saw the email before I'd even poured my coffee. My Meta access was already revoked. Eleven years in tech, four of them as a technical program manager on the Reality Labs team, and it ended with a calendar invite titled "Important Update" that I never got to attend.

I'm writing this exactly 90 days later, from a coffee shop in Austin where I've just signed an offer for a new role, and I want to share the recovery plan that kept me sane.

**Days 1–14: Grieve deliberately.** I gave myself two full weeks to feel terrible. I finished severance paperwork (16 weeks plus my February bonus prorated — I ran every number the first weekend so money anxiety wouldn't ambush me later). I deleted Slack from my phone, blocked former coworkers' celebratory posts, and walked the Lady Bird Lake loop every morning at the same time I used to log on. Routine is scaffolding.

**Days 15–45: Rebuild the scaffolding.** I updated my LinkedIn on March 20 and braced for pity. Instead, my notifications exploded. Eleven former colleagues messaged me within a week; six of them had also been cut. We started a Discord called "Laid Off and Loaded on Caffeine" and met virtually every Tuesday. Referrals came from that group, not from cold applications.

I applied to exactly 40 jobs. That's it. Quality over volume: every application had a tailored resume, and I chased a warm intro for 30 of them.

**Days 46–90: Interview like it's your job, because it is.** I did two mock interviews a week — one with my friend Priya, one recorded on Loom so I could cringe at my own filler words. I bombed a system design round at a fintech in April and let myself sulk for one evening, then wrote three pages of notes on what went wrong.

The offer I signed yesterday is for a program management role at a healthcare startup, 15% less base than Meta but with equity that might actually mean something. Honestly? I sleep better. My old team's projects lived under constant reorganization; this company has a roadmap that hasn't pivoted since January.

If you got that calendar invite in March, here's my whole plan in one sentence: budget your grief, ration your applications, and let your network carry you when your legs give out.

Ninety days. It's survivable. You're more than your badge access.
