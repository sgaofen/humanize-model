On January 1, 2025, I deleted Facebook and Instagram from my phone and decided to stay off both for six months. I didn’t announce it with a dramatic post. I simply told my close friends and family to text me instead.

At first, the habit was surprisingly physical. I would unlock my phone while waiting for coffee at Blue Bottle in Oakland, reach for Instagram, and then remember it was gone. The first week, I probably picked up my phone 30 times a day without a real reason. By February, that dropped noticeably. I started carrying a paperback instead—mostly novels, but also a gardening book that had been sitting untouched on my nightstand since 2023.

The biggest change was how much quieter my mind felt. I hadn’t realized how often I was comparing my ordinary Tuesday to someone else’s carefully edited vacation, kitchen renovation, or career announcement. Without that constant stream, I stopped feeling vaguely behind in my own life. I also noticed that I remembered more of what I read and listened to. Instead of scrolling before bed, I spent 20 minutes reading with my daughter, Maya, and we finished five chapter books together between January and June.

I worried that I would miss important news or lose touch with people. I did miss a few birthdays, and I learned that some friendships had mostly been maintained through likes and story replies. But I also had more real conversations. I called my college friend Daniel in Chicago twice, something we hadn’t done in years, and my sister and I started sending each other Sunday voice messages.

The hardest part was returning. On July 1, I reinstalled both apps, expecting to feel refreshed and disciplined. Instead, I fell back into old patterns within three days. I deleted them again, this time keeping Facebook only on my laptop for specific family groups.

Six months offline didn’t make me more productive or enlightened. It simply helped me notice what I was choosing—and what was choosing for me. I learned that I don’t actually miss social media very much. I miss certain people, and now I know to contact them directly.
