**Predicting 90-Day Hospital Readmission Using Gradient Boosting on Longitudinal EHR Data from MIMIC-IV**

**Background:** Unplanned readmissions within 90 days of discharge remain a costly and clinically significant outcome, yet most predictive models emphasize the 30-day window mandated by CMS reporting, leaving longer-horizon risk underexplored.

**Methods:** We extracted 51,238 adult inpatient admissions (2008–2019) from the MIMIC-IV v2.2 database (Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center, Boston, MA), excluding in-hospital deaths and hospice discharges, yielding 46,904 eligible index admissions. Ninety-day readmission was defined as any subsequent inpatient encounter within 90 days of discharge (positive rate: 21.4%, n=10,038). We engineered 187 features spanning demographics, Elixhauser comorbidity indices, admission acuity (SOFA, SAPS-II), laboratory trajectories over the final 48 hours of stay, medication counts at discharge, length of stay, and prior utilization (ED visits and admissions in the preceding 12 months). Patients were split by unique subject ID (70/15/15 train/validation/test) to prevent leakage across encounters. We trained and compared XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost classifiers, tuning hyperparameters via 5-fold cross-validated Bayesian optimization (100 trials, Optuna) on the training set, with early stopping on validation AUROC.

**Results:** The LightGBM model achieved the best held-out performance (AUROC 0.762, 95% CI 0.748–0.776; AUPRC 0.451; Brier score 0.142), outperforming a logistic regression baseline (AUROC 0.681) and marginally exceeding XGBoost (AUROC 0.754) and CatBoost (AUROC 0.758). SHAP analysis identified prior 12-month admission count, discharge hemoglobin, Elixhauser comorbidity score, and discharge disposition (skilled nursing vs. home) as the top predictors. Calibration was strong across risk deciles (Hosmer-Lemeshow p=0.34). At a threshold flagging the top 20% of predicted risk, the model captured 58.3% of true readmissions (sensitivity), with a positive predictive value of 47.1%.

**Conclusions:** Gradient boosting models trained on routinely collected MIMIC-IV variables provide clinically meaningful discrimination for 90-day readmission risk, suggesting utility for extending discharge-planning interventions beyond the conventional 30-day focus, pending external validation on non-ICU-weighted cohorts.
